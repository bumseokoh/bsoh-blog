The Piney Scent of Chukryeongsan: Namyangju and Gapyeong's Fragrant Pine Forest

Overview

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Jangseong-gun, Jeonnam-Gwangju Special Metropolitan City) · Public Nuri

Chukryeongsan straddles the border between Namyangju and Gapyeong in Gyeonggi Province, with a summit elevation of roughly 886–891m according to trail survey data. It’s home to the Chukryeongsan Natural Recreation Forest, famous for its Korean pine plantations, so you get to combine a forest bath with a proper hike. The overall difficulty is rated “easy” based on the registered trail, but the actual route to the summit involves a net elevation gain in the 500–600m range, making it a real hike that’s no walk in the park for beginners. It’s reachable by public transit or car for a day trip, and you can knock out the round-trip hike comfortably within a day.

Mountain Shape and Terrain

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Tour Photo Gallery (Jangseong-gun, Jeollanam-do) · KOGL

Chukryeongsan is more of a soil mountain than a rocky one — its character comes from gentle ridgelines and dense forest rather than dramatic rock peaks. That said, there are rocky sections scattered across the summit area and along the ridge that open up into nice viewpoints, with formations like Namibawi and Hwachaebong strung along the course. Chukryeongsan is often hiked as a standalone destination, but it’s just as commonly done as a traverse connecting to Seorisan and Jugeumsan along the ridge. Along that ridgeline you’ll find the main peaks of Jugeumsan (around 830–831m), Jugeumsan Summit (around 766m), Dokbawi (around 813m), Seorisan (around 847m), and Seorisan Summit (around 860m) — all part of one continuous mountain chain running along the Namyangju–Gapyeong border. At the top of Chukryeongsan itself stands the summit marker (around 891m), a popular spot for hikers to snap their obligatory summit photo.

Feeling Your Way Up the Mountain

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Tourist Photo Gallery (Jangseong-gun, Jeonnam-Gwangju Integrated Special City) · Public Nuri

The trail up Chukryeongsan sticks mostly to forest paths from the trailhead all the way to the summit, and in the stretches thick with Korean pine and fir, you really feel it — that sharp scent of phytoncides and the deep shade underneath. In summer, the dense canopy keeps the worst of the sun off you, so it’s a surprisingly cool hike even in the heat, and come fall, the ridge walk turns into something genuinely pretty with all the autumn color. From the summit and a few viewpoints along the ridge, you get sweeping views out over the mountain ranges of Namyangju and Gapyeong, and around Namibawi Rock or Hwachaebong Peak, there’s a nice stretch of exposed rock that opens things up with that classic airy, rocky-ridge feel. Overall, it’s a pretty typical “soil mountain” course — gentle dirt trails and forest paths for most of the way, with the grade kicking up only right before the top.

Course 1 — City bus to Chukryeongsan Natural Recreation Forest → Summit three-way junction

📏 6.15km ⛰️ 620m gain ⏱️ 2:34 🔁 Loop Difficulty ★★★★☆ ⤓ GPX

Route: City bus stop at Chukryeongsan Natural Recreation Forest → supermarket → Chukryeongsan Valley Garden → Chukryeongsan Natural Recreation Forest → wooden bench → summit three-way junction → Chukryeongsan summit (886.2m) → helipad → viewpoint → junction → Namibawi Rock → Campsite No. 1 → restrooms

Getting to the trailhead: This course starts right from the city bus stop, so it’s a great pick if you’re relying on public transit. City buses heading toward Namyangju and Gapyeong stop near the Chukryeongsan Natural Recreation Forest, so you don’t need a car to get here. That said, the buses don’t run all that frequently, so it’s worth checking the timetable before you head out.

What it’s like, and who it suits: The hike opens with a village-road feel, passing a supermarket and the valley garden, before easing gradually into forest trail — a gentle warm-up that lets you loosen up before the real climbing starts. Things get steeper once you hit the three-way junction just below the summit, and after the top, the descent takes you past the helipad, a viewpoint, and Namibawi Rock, with the views opening up nicely along the way. If you want a hike that’s doable start to finish on public transit alone, this is the course for you.

Course 2 — Chukryeongsan Recreation Forest → Summit

📏 6.87km ⛰️ 483m gain ⏱️ 2:31 🔁 Loop Difficulty ★★★★☆ ⤓ GPX

Route: Chukryeongsan Recreation Forest → Recreation Forest Parking Lot → Forest Recreation Hall → Fork in the trail → Fir Forest → Seori-san Helipad Shelter → Fir Grove → Recreation Forest Management Office → Recreation Forest Parking Lot No. 2 → Restroom at Parking Lot No. 2 → Bus-Only Parking Area → Chukryeongsan Recreation Forest Checkpoint → Forest House in the Recreation Forest

Getting to the trailhead: This course starts right at the front gate of the recreation forest, and since the forest itself has both a regular parking lot and a dedicated bus parking area, it’s convenient whether you’re driving yourself or coming with a chartered bus group. You’ll need to go through the admission/ticketing process to get in, so it’s worth checking the forest’s operating hours before you head out.

Highlights and who it’s for: The trail runs for a good stretch through the recreation forest’s facilities and planted woods — the Forest Recreation Hall, the fir forest, the fir grove — which makes it especially good for families or anyone looking to combine a hike with a bit of forest bathing. It’s the longest of the three courses by distance, but it also has the gentlest net elevation gain, so the climb feels less demanding than the numbers might suggest. The stretch past the Seori-san Helipad Shelter has more of a ridge-line feel, so it suits anyone who wants a mix of forest paths and ridge walking.

Course 3 — Junction 7 → Hwachaebong

📏 8.82km ⛰️ 601m gain ⏱️ 3:12 ➡️ One-way Difficulty ★★★★★ ⤓ GPX

Route: Junction 7 → Firefly Habitat → Junction 9 → Nature Shelter → Junction 11 → Junction 12 → Junction 13 → Ridge → Ridge Trail → Chungnyeongsan → Hwachaebong → Junction 19 → Azalea Garden → Junction 21 → Azalea Ridge

Getting to the trailhead: This is the longest of the three courses, threading through several junctions rather than starting from a single, clearly defined trailhead — it’s really more of an entry point onto the ridge traverse. Driving in and parking nearby works better than public transit here, and if it’s your first time, it’s worth checking the junction numbers on a map app beforehand so you don’t get turned around.

Highlights & who it’s for: This long ridge course takes you past the firefly habitat and the nature shelter, through a string of junctions up onto the ridge, then on to the summit of Chungnyeongsan before continuing to Hwachaebong and the Azalea Garden. The azalea garden section is especially gorgeous when the flowers are in bloom. Since this is the longest and most demanding of the three courses, it’s best suited to hikers with good stamina, or anyone aiming for a full ridge traverse.

Difficulty, Terrain & Cautions

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Jangseong County, Jeollanam-do) · KOGL

Chukryeongsan’s registered trail sections officially top out at “Easy,” but don’t let that fool you into underestimating the route — the actual summit climb, measured by elevation gain and distance, works out closer to a ★★★★☆ to ★★★★★ level. There are 8 confirmed viewpoints along the course, and one of them, marked “viewpoint, slightly hazardous,” calls for careful footing — don’t rush through it. The Cheoljjuk-dongsan (azalea garden) viewpoint, by contrast, is a spot where you can take in the view in relative safety. No section is officially flagged as a danger zone, but the rocky stretches around Namibawi Rock and Hwachaebong Peak get slippery in the rain or when they ice over in winter, so extra care is needed there. And since so much of the trail runs through forest, fallen leaves can pile up and make the ground slick from late fall into early winter.

Distance and Time Needed

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization photo gallery (Jangseong-gun, Jeollanam-do) · Public Nuri

Chukryeongsan’s registered trail network totals 7.85 km, but that’s a combined figure across multiple routes within the jurisdiction, not the length of any single course. There are two registered sections: an unnamed one at 4.67 km and the Oebang-ri section at 3.18 km. Going by actual measured summit routes, one-way distances run somewhere in the 6–9 km range, which makes for a perfectly manageable round-trip day hike. The recorded times across the whole trail network — 187 minutes up, 130 minutes down — work out to an average uphill pace of about 24 minutes per km, but that’s just a general pace reference rather than the time for any specific route. For actual course times, it’s more accurate to go by the one-way estimates in the table above (roughly 2 hours 30 minutes to 3 hours 12 minutes). There’s no shelter listed along the way, and the total trail length is on the shorter side, so Chukryeongsan is very much a there-and-back-in-a-day mountain — no overnight stay needed.

Getting There (Public Transit & Parking)

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Jangseong-gun, Jeollanam-do) · KOGL Type 1

For public transit, there are a few stops near Chungnyeongsan — Chungnyeongsan Natural Recreation Forest, Witmal, and Chungnyeongsan — reachable by city bus from the Namyangju or Gapyeong direction. Coming from Seoul, the usual route is to take the subway out to the Gyeongchun or Jungang Line area (Cheongnyangni, Sangbong, etc.) and transfer to an intercity or local bus from there. That said, definitely double-check the exact transfer routes and bus schedules on KakaoMap or Naver Map in real time, since they can change. If you’re driving, there’s a bus parking lot and a second parking lot near the Chungnyeongsan Natural Recreation Forest, so finding a spot generally isn’t a problem. It can get crowded on weekends and during peak season, though, so I’d recommend getting there early.

Season, Weather & Gear

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Jeongnam-Gwangju Special Self-Governing City, Jangseong-gun) · KOGL

Chukryeongsan is a mountain you can hike year-round, but because it’s so densely forested, summer humidity can make the trail feel a lot tougher than the numbers suggest, and in winter, shaded sections and rocky patches ice over easily — without crampons you’re asking to slip. Spring brings fresh new leaves and a beautiful spread of azaleas and royal azaleas, while fall covers the whole course in vivid foliage, making both seasons especially popular for hiking here. As for gear, you’ll want proper hiking boots, extra water (there’s basically no water source on the mountain, so bring enough), and a waterproof, insulating outer layer, plus crampons and gaiters if you’re going in winter. In summer, it’s worth packing bug repellent and wearing something breathable too.

Foreigner Traveler Tips

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Jangseong-gun, Gwangju) · KOGL Type 1

Chukryeongsan is romanized as “Chukryeongsan,” and the summit marker at the top is often labeled “Jeongsangseok (Summit Marker).” One of the trailheads, Chukryeongsan Natural Recreation Forest, is listed under that English name, so it’s worth searching for when you’re mapping out your route. You don’t need any special permit to hike the mountain itself, but if your route passes through the recreation forest, note that it has its own admission (ticketing) process and operating hours — worth checking before you go. Restrooms are available near Campsite No. 1 and near Parking Lot No. 2 at the recreation forest, but there’s no listed drinking water station on the mountain, so bring plenty of water with you. Google Maps tends to be unreliable for trail info on Korean mountains, so I’d recommend using KakaoMap or Naver Map instead — both give you the actual trail routes and public transit transfer details. Along the ridgeline and rocky sections, trail markers can be spotty, so if you’re hiking solo, keep an eye out so you don’t wander off-route. And if the weather turns bad, play it safe: heading back down beats pushing for the summit.

Nearby Peaks Worth a Look

  • Ununsan in Pocheon, Gyeonggi, with its Nunssseop and Byeongpung rock ridges — about 13 km away as the crow flies
  • Myeongjisan in Gapyeong, known for its twin-peak ridge and stunning fall foliage — about 22 km away as the crow flies
