Overview

The Geumgang Bike Path (금강자전거길, Geumgang Jajeongeo-gil) is one of the flagship national bike routes, running from the Geumgang Estuary Bank near the West Sea all the way upstream to Daecheong Dam inland. Wide river views, gently rolling farmland, and the cultural backdrop of Baekje’s old capitals combine to give you a calm, almost lyrical ride — the kind where the river never really leaves your side. Since it mostly follows flat riverside paths without any major climbs, it’s a long-haul route that riders of beginner-to-intermediate stamina can still take on, as long as they’ve got the endurance for the distance.

Here are a few ways you might tackle it.

  • Two-day through-ride: Given the one-way distance, the easiest approach is to stay overnight around Buyeo or Gongju and split the route across two days. Sleeping over in a Baekje heritage town turns the ride into a trip in its own right.
  • Day-trip by section: If riding the whole thing in one go feels like too much, you can break it into day-sized chunks between certification centers — say, “Estuary Bank to Baekjebo” or “Gongju–Sejong–Daecheong Dam.”
  • Family/beginner strolling section: The stretch from Sejongbo to Daecheong Dam has a lot of well-maintained, riverside-only paths, making it a good fit for mini-velos, Brompton folders, or short family rides.
  • Mini-velo + public transit combo: A popular option is loading a folding bike onto a train to reach the start or end point, then riding just one direction as a one-way through-ride.

Course Info

The route starts at the Geumgang Estuary Bank certification center in Seocheon/Gunsan. With the estuary bank — where sea meets river — behind you, you head upstream into the wetlands of the lower reaches, where reed fields and migratory bird habitats stretch out before you. From there, you pass the Seongdangpogu certification center in Iksan and enter Buyeo, a region steeped in the atmosphere of old Baekje.

Past Buyeo’s Baekjebo, the river widens noticeably, and you reach Gongju and its Gongjubo certification center — a stretch that runs alongside Gongsanseong Fortress and the lingering charm of an old capital. From there you pass through Sejongbo in the planned city of Sejong, follow a well-maintained urban riverside bike path upstream, and finally wrap up the journey at the Daecheongdam certification center further inland.

The surface is mostly dedicated riverside bike path and quite pleasant to ride, though a few stretches near urban entry points and bridge crossings are shared with pedestrians and vehicles. The route tends to trend gently uphill as you head upstream, but there’s hardly any steep climbing. Since this is a certification route, I’d recommend using the six certification centers as waypoints to track your progress along the way.

Nearby Eats

Every stretch of this route has its own distinct food scene, so it doubles nicely as a foodie trip alongside your resupply stops. Near the starting point, Gunsan is best known for jjamppong and other Chinese-Korean dishes from its old Chinese immigrant community, its historic bakeries, and West Sea seafood like bakdae (dried pollack) and blue crab. In the Buyeo–Gongju area — heart of the old Baekje kingdom — the go-to dishes are usually lotus-leaf rice, hanjeongsik (Korean set meals), and gukbap (rice-and-soup dishes). As you head toward Sejong and Daejeon, restaurants and cafés get a lot more plentiful, making resupply that much easier.

That said, the riverside stretches between the downstream wetlands and the weirs can have long gaps with no convenience stores or restaurants, so it’s smart to stock up well on water and snacks before you leave the city behind. Rather than chasing specific shops, a simpler rule of thumb works better: handle your meals and resupply whenever you roll into a town with a certification center. Stick to that, and you’re unlikely to go wrong.

Supplies & Amenities

This route has 6 certification centers, 84 restrooms, 26 water stations, and 3 air pumps along the way. For a long-distance route, the restrooms and water stations are packed in fairly tight, so it’s easy to plan out your water refills and rest stops. Air pumps are another story, though — with only 3 of them scattered along the route, you shouldn’t count on finding one nearby. Bring a portable pump or a CO₂ inflator just in case.

Since this is a certified route, it’s worth carrying the Bike Passport (국토종주 자전거길 여행 수첩) so you can stamp it at each certification center as proof of completion. The centers come in two flavors: staffed booths and unmanned stamp booths shaped like red telephone booths. At the unmanned ones, you just grab the stamp sitting there and press it into your passport yourself. Fill in all 6 and you’ve got your section completion verified.

Return Trip Planning

Since this is a 146km one-way route, it’s worth working out how you’ll get back to your starting point before you set off.

  • Riding back the way you came (roughly 292km round trip): If you’re a long-distance rider with the legs and the schedule to spare, retracing your route is doable, but it’s too much for a single day — plan on at least 2 nights.
  • Taking the train: The most practical option is heading back by train from Sintanjin Station or Daejeon Station, both near Daecheong Dam at the endpoint. Regular trains like the Mugunghwa and Saemaeul tend to be fairly flexible about bringing bikes aboard, while KTX usually requires folding bikes to be broken down and packed to fit the baggage rules. Either way, check the specific train’s bike policy before you board.
  • Connecting by bus: Near the endpoint, the Daecheong Dam and Geumgang Nature Center stops are useful reference points for your return. That said, intercity and express buses may or may not take bikes depending on the route and the driver’s discretion, so unless you’re riding a folding bike, the train is the safer bet for getting back.

Safety Tips

  • Shared pedestrian/road sections: Near the downtown entrance and around bridge approaches, you’ll be sharing the path with pedestrians and traffic. Slow down and let people know you’re there with your bell or a quick call-out.
  • Ice and wet surfaces: Given the riverside setting, early mornings, shaded winter stretches, and the bridges themselves can get icy or frosty and slippery.
  • Night lights: This is a long ride, so you may well finish after dark. Front and rear lights are a must — a lot of the riverside path has few, if any, streetlights.
  • Hydration: Especially in summer, it’s easy to get dehydrated on stretches with no water stations. Scope out where the refill points are beforehand and carry plenty of water.

Season, Weather & Gear

The best seasons are spring and fall, when temperatures are mild and the scenery really shines. Summer along the river brings brutal radiant heat and almost no shade, so heat exhaustion and dehydration become real concerns, while winter cranks up the difficulty with riverside winds and icy patches. Regardless of season, the riverside tends to be windy, so pacing yourself with headwinds in mind really matters.

For essential gear, I’d recommend a helmet, front and rear lights, and a flat kit (spare tube, tire levers, and either a mini pump or CO₂ inflator), plus plenty of water and snacks. Since this is a long-distance ride, padded cycling shorts, a spare top, and a thin windbreaker jacket all come in handy too.

Tips for Foreign Travelers

Here’s everything you need to get ready, all in one place.

① Route name (Romanized): Geumgang Bike Path — Geumgang Jajeongeo-gil. Key points along the way include the Geumgang Estuary Bank, Seongdangpogu, Baekjebo, Gongjubo, Sejongbo, and Daecheongdam.

② A practical guide to the certification system: This route is part of Korea’s national cross-country cycling certification program.

  • Bike Passport: Your best bet is buying one in person at a staffed certification center. The official reservation site is Korean-only, and overseas cards plus Korean real-name verification make advance purchase tricky for most foreign visitors — just pick one up at one of the larger centers when you arrive.
  • Unmanned stamp booths: You’ll spot these along the route — they’re shaped like little red British phone booths. Just use the stamp inside to mark the matching box in your passport.
  • Finishing rewards: Collect stamps from all 6 certification centers to complete the section, and you’ll typically get a certification sticker, a completion certificate, and a medal.

③ Coming without a bike: You can rent one locally, or if you’re in Seoul, use the public bike-share system Ttareungi. Ttareungi has an English app and takes overseas cards, but it’s Seoul-only, so it won’t help you once you’re actually on the Geumgang route. If you’re starting near the route itself, it’s worth looking up local rental shops ahead of time.

④ Map apps: Google Maps’ cycling directions aren’t very reliable in Korea. I’d strongly recommend using KakaoMap or Naver Map instead — both support bike mode. They both have English interfaces too, and their bike paths, elevation info, and distance guidance are far more accurate.
