Overview

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization photo gallery (Yeongbuk-myeon, Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do) · KOGL Type 1

Myeongseongsan (鳴聲山) straddles the border between Galmal-eup in Cheorwon County, Gangwon-do, and Idong-myeon/Yeongbuk-myeon in Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do. It’s best known for the way it rises above Sanjeongho Lake to its south — a silhouette that’s become something of a signature view. The summit is officially listed at 943m on topographic maps, though the highest point actually measured along this particular route came in at 926m, so there’s some discrepancy depending on exactly where you take the reading. The mountain is especially famous for its autumn silver grass fields, and the overall course is rated “easy” in the data, meaning even beginner or intermediate hikers can knock it out as a comfortable day hike. That said, the Samgakbong section near the summit mixes rocky ridgelines with steep pitches, so how hard it actually feels can vary quite a bit section to section.

Ridgeline and Terrain

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization photo gallery (Yeongbuk-myeon, Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do) · KOGL

Myeongseongsan is a classic mix of soil mountain and rocky peaks. Most of the mountain is made up of gentle ridgelines and broad slopes covered in silver grass, but as you climb closer to the summit, rocky ridges and craggy peaks start to take over. The main peaks include the Myeongseongsan silver grass field (763m), where the grass spreads out widest; Myeongseongsan Summit (943m), known as the mountain’s top; the actual surveyed peak of Myeongseongsan (767m); and Samgakbong (roughly 911–913m, with slight variation depending on the source) along the ridge west of the summit. There are also nearby peaks like Mangmubong (454m, sometimes marked as 440m on trail signs), which link up along the ridge and make for a fun multi-peak traverse if you’re doing the whole line. Down in the valley you’ll find waterfall terrain like Deungryong Falls, so think of it as a hike with two distinct halves: a valley walk down low, and silver-grass ridges with rocky scrambles up top.

Feel of the Hike

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization photo gallery (Yeongbuk-myeon, Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do) · KOGL (Korea Open Government License)

Starting from the Sanjeongho Lake trailhead, the first stretch is an easy, gently sloping forest path that winds past the lakeside shops and a walking trail. From there, the route follows a valley up through the Deungnyong Falls section, where you’re walking under thick forest shade with the sound of water beside you the whole way. Once you pass the rest area, the view starts opening up. By the time you reach the Silver Grass Wind Trail and its photo-spot section, you’re out on a fully open ridge blanketed in silver grass — come autumn, this stretch turns into the mountain’s signature photo spot, famous for its silvery, rippling fields. Past the octagonal pavilion and on toward the summit, the trail turns into a steep, rocky scramble that really starts to burn your legs — there are a few spots where you’ll need to use your hands, adding a different kind of tension than the lower part of the hike. Seasonally, this mountain is known for the cool shade and waterfalls along the valley in summer, the silver grass in autumn, and in winter, the biting wind on the ridge along with the snow-covered grass fields — each one memorable in its own way.

Getting to the trailhead: The Myeongseongsan trailhead sits near the Sangdong parking lot by Sanjeongho Lake, so most hikers approach through the Sanjeongho Lake tourist area. If you’re coming from Seoul or elsewhere in the greater metro area by public transit, the usual route is an intercity bus toward Cheorwon or Pocheon, getting off near Sanjeongho Lake and walking the rest of the way. If you’re driving, the parking lot at the Sanjeongho Lake tourist complex is the go-to option.

Highlights and who it suits: This route opens with an easy stroll through the Sanjeongho Lake tourist area, then follows a valley trail past Deungnyong Falls before climbing the silvergrass ridge to the rocky summit of Samgakbong — a classic traverse-style route on Myeongseongsan. The lower half is gentle, but the elevation gain picks up sharply higher up, where you’ll hit some rocky ridge sections. It’s a great pick if you want to enjoy the autumn silvergrass scenery, but you’ll want a reasonable level of fitness and hiking experience to handle the upper stretch comfortably. This one’s better suited to intermediate or more experienced hikers than to beginners.

Difficulty, Trail Conditions & Cautions

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do) · KOGL Type 1

All four registered sections in this district are rated “easy,” so trail conditions overall are pretty manageable. That said, the measured summit route (Myeongseongsan trailhead → Samgakbong) climbs 684m over 8.55km, earning a full 5-star difficulty rating — this is a leg-burner. The rocky ridge section near Samgakbong in particular gets slick when wet, so watch your footing in rain, and in winter the icing risk is high enough that I wouldn’t head in without crampons. There are 3 confirmed viewpoints along the route, with the stretch between Deungnyong Falls and the silver grass wind path, plus the area around the octagonal pavilion, offering nice views toward Sanjeong Lake. No sections are specifically flagged as hazard zones, so sticking to the marked trail keeps things reasonably safe — but there are no registered shelters, so if the weather turns, it’s best to head down early rather than push on.

Distance and Time

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization photo gallery (Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do) · KOGL

Adding up every trail segment in the area, the recorded ascent time comes to 461 minutes and the descent to 323 minutes. Converted against the total trail length of 22.09km, that works out to an average ascent pace of roughly 21 minutes/km. Keep in mind this figure is a sum across all the trails in the area combined — it shouldn’t be mistaken for the time on any single route. The actual summit route, from the Myeongseongsan entrance to Samgakbong (8.55km, 684m of net elevation gain), is calculated at about 3 hours 17 minutes one way. For a round trip including the descent, plan on roughly 5 to 6 hours total. Given that the area’s trails add up to just over 22km with no shelters along the way, this is a mountain you can comfortably knock out in a single day hike, no overnight stay required.

Getting there (public transit & parking)

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do) · KOGL

Near the trailhead on the Sanjeong Lake side of Myeongseongsan, there’s a confirmed bus stop called “Sanjeong Lake·Sangdong Parking Lot” (산정호수상동주차장), so the usual approach is to take an intercity or local bus heading toward Cheorwon or Pocheon, get off there, and walk the rest of the way to the trail entrance. Coming from the Seoul metro area, you can catch an intercity bus bound for Cheorwon from Dong Seoul Bus Terminal, or transfer onto a bus that routes through Pocheon. That said, this dataset doesn’t show a registered parking lot for this trailhead — most visitors end up using the public parking areas within the Sanjeong Lake tourist district, so it’s worth checking KakaoMap or Naver Map for “Sanjeong Lake” (산정호수) or “Myeongseongsan trail entrance” (명성산등산로입구) before you go, to see real-time road conditions and where parking is actually available.

Season, Weather, and Gear

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization Photo Gallery (Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do) · KOGL Type 1

Myeongseongsan gets the biggest crowds during the fall silver grass season (roughly late September to mid-October), when the Silvergrass Wind Trail and the silver grass photo zone can get seriously packed. Spring and fall are the easiest seasons for hiking here. In summer, watch out for the humidity and heat along the valley sections, plus the chance of washed-out trails during monsoon downpours. Winter brings harsh, biting winds along the ridgeline, and the rocky ridge sections can ice over badly, so cold-weather gear, crampons, and gaiters are all but essential. Across seasons, you’ll want ankle-supporting hiking boots, plenty of water (there are no confirmed water stations on the trail, so bring what you need), trekking poles, and a rain jacket or windbreaker.

Tips for foreign travelers

Photo: Korea Tourism Organization photo gallery (Pocheon, Gyeonggi Province) · KOGL

The summit is romanized as Myeongseongsan Peak, the nearby secondary peak as Samgakbong, and the lake below as Sanjeongho Lake. Since this isn’t a national park, there’s no separate entry permit or admission fee to worry about — though some facilities within the Sanjeongho Lake tourist district do charge their own fees. Restrooms are available near the trailhead (at the Sanjeongho Lake public restroom), but there don’t appear to be any restrooms or water fountains along the trail itself, so it’s worth using the facilities and stocking up on water before you set off. As for maps, Google Maps often falls short when it comes to Korean trails and footpaths, so I’d recommend installing KakaoMap or Naver Map instead — they’ll give you accurate trail routes and public transit info. For safety, it’s best to avoid hiking the rocky ridge sections near the summit alone; go with a companion if you can, and build in enough time to finish your descent before sunset.
